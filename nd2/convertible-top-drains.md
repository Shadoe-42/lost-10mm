## Safety

- **The top has to be partly open to reach the filters, so treat the frame as a pinch point.** The work happens in tight spots near the top's frame. Only the adult moves the top. Once it is where you need it, nobody touches it, the latches, or the frame until every hand is out and the filters are back in.
- **Close and latch the top only when every hand is clear.** Mazda says to stop in a safe place before operating the top, and that applies here. Count hands before you move it.
- **Use the park position.** Level ground, engine off, parking brake on.
- **Keep the rear window defogger off.** Mazda warns that the heat from the defogger can damage the top when it is open. It is a good habit to keep it off whenever you work around the top.
- **Do not force anything.** The filters release with a tab. If one does not come out when you press it, stop and look. A broken tab means water in the cabin later.

## Why this exists

A soft top does not make a car waterproof by being sealed shut. It sheds water. Rain runs over the fabric, collects in channels along the edges, and leaves the car through drains. The drain filters sit in those paths (⚠️ whether each side holds one filter or more is still to be confirmed on our car) and catch leaves, grit, and pollen before they can clog the outlets. That is their whole job. The water leaves the car through drains under the car, just inboard of the door sills.

When a filter loads up, the water has nowhere to go, so it backs up and goes the other way. Mazda states the consequence plainly: if leaves or other matter block the drain filter, water may enter the vehicle. A clogged drain does not show up until it rains hard, and then it shows up inside the car. That is why the job is calendar-driven and not condition-driven. You will not notice a partly blocked filter by looking at the car. You find it by reaching in and cleaning it before the weather does.

Spring and fall are the natural times. Fall catches the leaves of the season, which are the main enemy, and spring clears out what winter left behind and gets the car ready for a season of open-top driving. Mazda sets a floor of once a year. Twice a year costs very little and is better.

## What healthy looks like

- **Every filter sits firmly in place and its tab locks.** A loose filter does not seal and is a leak path.
- **The filters are clean or nearly clean.** A light film of dust is normal. A packed mat of leaves or mud is not.
- **The openings behind the filters are clear.** Look with a flashlight for debris, standing water, or a dark stain that shows water has been sitting.
- **The cabin and trunk stay dry after rain and after washing.** No damp carpet, no stained trim, no musty smell.

## Old versus new

The filters are small enough to put side by side, so set the one you took out next to a clean one if you have a spare, or next to the photo in the owner's manual.

- **New or healthy:** clean, intact, a tab that snaps and locks, and openings you can see through.
- **Worn out or needs work:** a cracked or chipped body, a tab that no longer locks, filter material that is crumbling or packed solid, or a loose fit. Replace any filter that does not seat and lock. ⚠️ Photos of a clean filter and of one loaded with debris belong here once we have them.

## Steps

1. **Pick the day.** A dry day, parked level with the engine off. Gather a flashlight, a soft brush or cloth, and an air source with a regulator.
2. **Open the top part way and find drain filter A.** The filters are reached with the top partly open. Mazda's manual does not describe that and says only to find filter A by hand from behind the bow, so this is what works on our car. Open it only as far as you need, and then nobody touches the top until the filters are back in. Filter A is on the driver's side. Reach in from behind the bow, behind the seat, and feel for it. Mazda's manual shows it as a small molded piece with a tab.
3. **Press the tab and remove filter A.** Press the tab and lift the filter out of the driver's side.
4. **Remove filter B on the passenger's side.** Reach to the other side and lift it out of its opening. Mazda's manual shows it as a small block with holes in it. The manual's wording suggests B sits in the opening behind A. On our car A and B are on opposite sides, and we describe what is on our car. ⚠️ One community write-up describes a hard plastic filter and a sponge-type filter together on each side, which would match the manual's pairing. We will confirm which it is on the next clean.
5. **Clear the debris.** Pull the baskets, as many owners call the filters, and take the leaves, grit, and whatever else out of them.
6. **Look at the opening and clear the path.** Shine the flashlight into each opening and check that it is clear. Remove anything you can reach. Then run a short burst of low-pressure compressed air through the system to help clear what you cannot reach. We keep it to about 2 bar (about 29 psi) at most. ⚠️ Mazda does not describe this step, so treat it as our practice. Keep the pressure low, and do not aim it at the fabric, the seals, or the seams.
7. **Reinstall in reverse.** Put each filter back in its own opening, and press each one until its tab locks. Mazda says water may enter the vehicle if a filter is not secured in its position, so check by feel that every one is locked.
8. **Check that both sides are done.** A is the driver's side and B is the passenger's, so confirm that both are finished and seated before you close and latch the top.
9. **Log it.** Write down the date, what you found, and how loaded the filters were.

## While you are at the top: what to leave alone

Mazda lists a few cautions that belong with this job, because they protect the same seals.

- **Avoid automatic and high-pressure car washes.** Mazda says both are harmful to the top.
- **Do not spray water directly at the glass-to-top junction or the seam between the body and the top.** Water can get through into the cabin and trunk.
- **Wash by hand.** Mazda says to remove loose dust with a soft brush, use a neutral synthetic detergent, plenty of water, and a soft brush, rinse well, dry as much as you can, and let the top dry completely before lowering it.
- **No wax on the fabric.** If you get some on it, Mazda says to remove it with a good leather cleaner or a mild detergent solution of about 5 percent.
- **No alcohol, bleach, or solvents.** Mazda warns that thinner, benzene, gasoline, and the like can discolor or stain the top.

## When it goes wrong

- **Water in the cabin or trunk after rain or washing.** Check the drain filters first. A packed or unseated filter is the most common cause. If they are clean and seated, look at the seals and the seam.
- **A filter will not come out.** Do not pry. Press the tab again, and check the angle. Look with a flashlight.
- **A filter will not lock back in.** The tab may be bent or the opening may have debris in it. Clean the opening, and replace the filter if the tab is damaged.
- **A broken or missing filter.** Replace it before the next rain, and do not drive in rain without it.
- **Debris keeps coming back.** Look at where it comes from. Leaves under a tree, or in the cowl, will refill the filters in a season.

## Log it

Write down the date, the mileage if you like, what each filter looked like when you took it out, and whether the cabin and trunk stayed dry. Set the next date for the opposite season.
