## Safety

- **Never work under a car held up by a jack alone.** Use jack stands or a lift. Mazda's manual shows jacking positions for the factory jack only, and it does not publish stand or lift points in the text. ⚠️ We have not yet verified stand and lift points for this guide.
- **Level matters twice.** A car that tilts gives a false fill level, and a car that tilts on stands is a car that moves. Check both before you start.
- **Hot gear oil burns.** Warm oil drains faster and carries more suspended material out with it, but warm does not mean hot. Drive a few minutes, then let the car sit a few minutes before you open anything.
- **Gear oil has a strong smell and stains.** Wear gloves and eye protection, and keep rags handy. Wipe spills before they reach the exhaust or the tires.
- **Keep helpers out from under a lifted car.** Anyone small enough to wander under it stays clear until the car is back on the ground.
- **Used oil is hazardous waste.** Take it to a parts store or waste facility that accepts it. Do not pour it down a drain or onto the ground.

## Why this exists

A manual gearbox is a pile of gears, shafts, bearings, and synchronizers, all of which survive because a film of oil keeps them from touching. The oil does three jobs at once. It lubricates the load-bearing surfaces, it carries away heat, and it holds the fine metal particles that every gearbox sheds during break-in and normal wear until you drain them out.

The rear differential has a harder life than it looks. Its hypoid gears slide as they roll, which loads the oil film heavily, so the oil carries extra-pressure additives. Those additives, and the oil itself, wear out with heat and shear. The case holds only 0.6 L, which is a small reserve to share across that much work.

That is why Mazda gives the transmission a calendar number as well as a mileage number, and why the Schedule 2 interval is half of Schedule 1. Time ages gear oil even when the car sits, because heat cycles and moisture work on the additives whether or not the gears are turning. This is the same logic as the brake fluid rule: if you drive a few days a week, the calendar usually arrives before the odometer does.

The differential is the interesting disagreement. Mazda lists no replacement interval for it and says to replace the oil only when the component has been submerged in water. Many owners change it anyway, because 0.6 L is cheap and the job is small. We lay out both positions and do not pick one for you. ⚠️ Our own practice goes here once confirmed.

## What healthy looks like

Learn this baseline first. Every other section depends on it.

- **The housings are dry.** A little grime around a plug is normal. A wet, shiny film with dust sticking to it is a slow leak.
- **Shifts are smooth.** Each gear goes in without a notch or a crunch, cold and warm. A gearbox that feels different on a cold morning than it did last month is telling you something.
- **No whine or grinding.** A faint transmission sound in a gear is common. A new whine from the rear on a steady cruise is worth a look at the differential.
- **The oil at the fill hole.** When you open the fill plug the oil should sit near the lower edge of the hole, look like clean oil, and smell like gear oil. Burnt, sharp, or chemical smells are a warning.
- **Fine dust on a magnetic plug is normal.** If your drain plug is magnetic (⚠️ confirm on the car), a thin gray paste is ordinary wear. Chips, flakes, or anything you can pick up between your fingers is not.

## Old versus new

Gear oil is the one part of this job you can set side by side. Pour a little of the oil that comes out of the car into a clear jar, and put it next to a splash from the freshly opened bottle.

- **New:** clean and translucent, with no sheen of metal and no cloudiness.
- **Worn out:** darker, thicker-looking, possibly cloudy or milky if water got in, with a burnt smell. A glittery metallic sheen means metal is leaving the gears.

Color is a prompt, not a measurement. Gear oil can be worn before it looks dark, so the calendar decides when to change it and the jar only confirms what the calendar told you. ⚠️ Photos of the jar comparison and of the drain plug belong here once we have them.

## Steps

Do the transmission first, then the differential, and complete each unit before you start the other.

1. **Check what you have.** Both fluids, both plug washers if needed, and a transfer pump that fits the fill hole. Do not open anything until you can close it again.
2. **Warm the oil a little.** A short drive is enough. Then park on level ground.
3. **Lift the car and set it level.** Use stands or the lift. Give the car a firm shove to confirm it is stable before you get under it. ⚠️ Stand and lift points to be confirmed.
4. **Open the fill plug first.** Always. If the fill plug will not open, you want to learn that while the gearbox is still full, not after you have drained it. Loosen it, then leave it loosely in place for now. ⚠️ Fill plug location and socket size on the transmission to be confirmed and photographed.
5. **Drain the transmission.** Place the pan, remove the drain plug, and let it drain until it slows to a drip. Look at the oil and, if the plug is magnetic, at what is stuck to it. ⚠️ Drain plug location and socket size to be confirmed.
6. **Close the drain.** Clean the plug and its seat, fit a new washer or gasket if the plug uses one, and tighten to specification. ⚠️ Torque to be confirmed from Mazda service information.
7. **Fill the transmission.** Pump fresh Mazda Original Long Life Gear Oil IS in through the fill hole until oil begins to seep back out of it. The level is right when the oil sits at the lower edge of the hole. Capacity is about 2.0 L, and that number is a cross-check, not a target. The fill hole decides.
8. **Close the fill plug.** Clean it, refit with a new washer if it uses one, and tighten to specification. ⚠️ Torque to be confirmed.
9. **Repeat for the rear differential.** Fill plug first, then drain, then close the drain, then fill with Mazda Long Life Hypoid Gear Oil SG1 to the lower edge of the hole (about 0.6 L), then close the fill plug. ⚠️ Locations, sizes, and torque to be confirmed.
10. **Wipe everything and lower the car.** Clean the housings so a new leak will show up against a clean surface.
11. **Test.** Drive gently. Shift through every gear, including reverse, and listen for noise from the rear. Let the car sit afterwards and look under it for drips.
12. **Check again.** After a few days of driving, look at both plugs and at the ground beneath the car.

## When it goes wrong

- **A plug will not open.** Stop. Use the correct size, a six-point tool, and steady force, not impact. If the fill plug is stuck and the oil is already drained, you have a car that cannot be driven. This is why the fill plug opens first.
- **Notchy or stiff shifting after the change.** Check the fluid level first, then check that the right fluid went in. Mazda notes that very low temperatures can make shifting difficult with the alternative GL-4 oil.
- **A new whine from the rear.** Check the differential level and look for a leak at the axle seals or the pinion seal. Persistent whine is a case for a shop.
- **Oil weeping at a plug.** Usually the washer or the torque. Wipe, retorque to specification, and watch it.
- **Overfilled.** Oil will run out of the fill hole as soon as you open it. Let it drain until it stops, then close. Underfilled is the more dangerous mistake, so err toward seeing oil at the hole.
- **Oil is milky.** Water got in. Drain, refill, and find out where the water came from. Mazda says to replace differential oil whenever the component has been submerged.

## Log it

Write down the date, the mileage, the fluid brand and type, how much came out, and what the old oil looked like. Then set the next date for the transmission using the calendar number, whichever of time or miles comes first.
