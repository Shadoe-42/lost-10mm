## Safety

- **Never work under a car held up by a jack alone.** Use jack stands or a lift. Mazda's manual shows jacking positions for the factory jack only, and does not publish stand or lift points in the text. ⚠️ We have not yet verified stand and lift points for this guide.
- **Hot oil burns.** Warm oil drains faster and carries more suspended material out with it, but warm does not mean hot. Drive for a few minutes, then let the car sit a few minutes before you open anything. The exhaust is near the work area, so mind your arms.
- **Slow down at the filter.** It is full of oil, it is usually reached from below, and a loose filter drops fast. Keep the drain pan under it, and keep your face out of the line of fire.
- **Wear gloves and eye protection.** Used oil is a skin irritant, and a splash in the eye is a bad afternoon.
- **Keep helpers out from under a lifted car.** Anyone small enough to wander under it stays clear until the car is back on the ground.
- **Used oil and filters are hazardous waste.** Take them to a parts store or waste facility that accepts them. Do not pour oil down a drain or onto the ground.

## Why this exists

Engine oil does four jobs at once. It lubricates the surfaces that slide against each other, it carries away heat that coolant cannot reach, it holds soot and wear particles in suspension until the filter catches them, and it helps seal the piston rings against the cylinder walls. None of that stops when the car sits still. The oil in a car that is driven a few days a week is not worn the way it would be after the same miles of steady highway driving, but it does age.

The reasons are time and short trips. Cold starts and short drives never get the engine hot enough, or long enough, to cook off the moisture and the small amount of fuel that reach the oil. That water and fuel stay in the oil, they thin it and attack its additives, and the oil does not get to repair itself. This is the same reasoning behind the brake fluid and the transmission rules: the calendar matters as much as the odometer, and a car that sits is not a car that ages gently.

Mazda knows this. On the flexible setting the car calculates remaining oil life from how the engine has been run and lights a wrench indicator when a change is due. Even so, the manual sets a ceiling: the oil and filter must be changed at least once a year or within 7,500 miles of the last change, and the engine oil data must be reset after every change. If the car is used the way Schedule 2 describes, you follow the fixed setting instead, at 6 months or 5,000 miles. Our 6-month rule sits on that fixed setting, and our mileage range sits inside it.

The filter is half the job. The oil carries the dirt, and the filter is where the dirt goes. Changing the oil and leaving the old filter in puts clean oil through a filter that is already loaded, and the filter holds a good part of a quart of old oil besides.

## What healthy looks like

Learn this baseline first. Every other section depends on it.

- **The oil level sits between MIN and MAX on the dipstick.** Mazda gives a procedure for reading it: level ground, engine warm, engine off for at least 5 minutes so the oil returns to the pan, wipe the dipstick, push it in fully, pull it out again, and read it. Check that the dipstick O-ring is seated before you push it back in.
- **The oil is amber to brown and translucent.** Oil darkens as it works, because detergent additives hold soot in suspension, so dark does not mean bad. Fresh oil is clear and amber.
- **No oil on the outside of the engine.** A little grime around the filter and drain plug is normal. A wet, shiny film with dust sticking to it is a slow leak.
- **No oil light.** The low oil pressure warning should never come on during normal driving.
- **The level barely moves between changes.** A slow, small drop over thousands of miles is common. A big drop is a reason to look.

## Old versus new

Oil is the one part of this job you can set side by side. Pour a little of the oil that comes out of the car into a clear jar, and put it next to a splash from the freshly opened bottle.

- **New:** clear, amber, and thin enough to see through.
- **Worn out:** darker, thicker-looking, possibly gritty between your fingers. Look for a glittery metallic sheen, a milky or foamy look, or a fuel smell, which point to metal wear, coolant or moisture, and fuel dilution in that order. ⚠️ Photos of the jar comparison belong here once we have them.

Color is a prompt, not a measurement. Oil can be tired before it looks dark, so the calendar and the monitor decide when to change it, and the jar only confirms what they told you.

The filter has its own comparison. Set the old filter next to the new one before you throw it away. The old one is heavy, dirty on the outside, and may have a stained or hardened gasket. The new one is light, clean, and has a soft, supple rubber ring.

## Steps

1. **Check what you have.** Oil, filter, washer if needed, a funnel, a pan big enough for the whole capacity, and the right tools for the drain plug and the filter. Do not open anything until you can close it again.
2. **Warm the oil a little.** A short drive is enough. Then park on level ground and let the car sit a few minutes.
3. **Lift the car and set it level.** Use stands or the lift. Give the car a firm shove to confirm it is stable before you get under it. ⚠️ Stand and lift points to be confirmed, and whether an underbody panel has to come off to reach the drain plug and filter.
4. **Take the filler cap off the engine.** It lets air in so the oil drains cleanly. It is shown in the engine compartment overview in the owner's manual.
5. **Drain the oil.** Place the pan under the drain plug. Remove the plug and let it drain until it slows to a drip. Look at the oil and look at the plug. ⚠️ Drain plug location and socket size to be confirmed.
6. **Remove the old filter.** Move the pan under it. Loosen it with the wrench and unscrew it by hand, keeping it upright as long as you can. Check that its rubber gasket came off with it. If the old gasket stays on the engine and a second filter goes on top, the two gaskets do not seal, and the oil goes everywhere. ⚠️ Filter location and wrench size to be confirmed.
7. **Clean the mating surfaces.** Wipe the engine's filter seat and the drain plug seat with a clean rag. Keep dirt out of the openings.
8. **Prepare the new filter.** Wet its rubber gasket with a little fresh oil. Some people also fill the filter part way, which helps with the first moments after startup. ⚠️ Whether to pre-fill is a community habit we have not verified for this engine.
9. **Install the new filter.** Thread it on by hand until the gasket touches, then tighten as the filter's instructions say. Do not use a wrench to tighten it, because overtightening damages the gasket and makes the next change harder. ⚠️ Confirm the filter's tightening instruction when we choose a part.
10. **Close the drain.** Fit a new washer if the plug uses one, thread the plug by hand, and tighten to specification. ⚠️ Torque to be confirmed from Mazda service information.
11. **Lower the car and fill.** Put the car on level ground and pour in most of the capacity, then stop. Mazda's figure is 4.3 L (4.5 US qt) with a new filter. Treat that as a cross-check and let the dipstick decide.
12. **Check the level.** Wait a few minutes, pull the dipstick, wipe it, push it in fully, and read it. Add a little at a time until the level sits at MAX. Do not overfill, because Mazda warns that too much oil can cause engine damage.
13. **Start and look.** Start the engine and let it idle for about half a minute. The oil pressure light should go out within a moment, and you should see no drip at the filter or the plug. If the light stays on, shut the engine off at once.
14. **Recheck the level.** Shut off the engine, wait at least 5 minutes, and read the dipstick again. Top up if needed.
15. **Reset the oil data.** Mazda says to reset it after every change, whatever the wrench light shows. ⚠️ The reset procedure is in the Mazda Connect manual, which we do not have in the repo. Add it here once confirmed on the car.
16. **Look under the car after your first drive.** Check the plug and the filter for weeping, and recheck the level.

## When it goes wrong

- **Oil pressure light comes on while driving.** Pull off the road onto level ground, shut off the engine, and wait 5 minutes. Check the level. If it is low, add oil without overfilling. Start the engine and watch the light. Mazda says that if the light stays on even with a normal level, stop the engine immediately and have the car towed. Do not keep running the engine with the light on.
- **Oil weeping at the filter.** The gasket is not seated, or a second gasket is still on the engine. Stop, find out, and fix it before you drive.
- **Oil weeping at the drain plug.** A washer that was not replaced or a plug that is not tight. Wipe it, retorque to specification, and watch it.
- **Overfilled.** The oil level reads above MAX. Mazda warns that this can damage the engine, so drain a little at the plug before you drive.
- **Oil on the exhaust smells and smokes after a change.** A spill. Wipe what you can reach, and watch for it to burn off. If it keeps going, look for a leak.
- **Milky oil or a fuel smell.** Moisture or fuel in the oil. If it shows up on a short-trip car, a longer drive helps. If it is milky on the dipstick or under the cap, get it looked at, because coolant may be getting in.

## Log it

Write down the date, the mileage, the oil brand and grade, the filter part number, how much came out, and what the old oil looked like. Reset the oil data, then set the next date six months out. Mazda's warranty language says that claims resulting from lack of maintenance will not be honored, and that parts and fluids must meet its standards, so a log with receipts is worth keeping.
